## Independence is the whole distinction

Three components sit next to each other, and picking between them is one question each:

- `Checkbox` — is this one setting independent of the others? Then a box.
- `Radio` — is exactly one of several answers allowed? Then a group of options.
- `Switch` — does flipping it take effect now, rather than on submit? Then a switch.

A column of checkboxes with only one allowed is a radio group that lets readers break it. A checkbox
that saves the moment it is ticked is a switch that lied about when it acts.

## `indeterminate` still submits as unchecked

There is no third value. The box shows a mixed state, announces one, and submits as unchecked —
because that is what the platform does, and inventing a third value would break every form that
reads the box.

It is a DOM property with no matching attribute, which is why it is a prop here at all: a consumer
cannot set it from JSX without a ref of their own.

Use it for the parent of a list where some children are ticked. Do not use it to mean "unknown" —
the reader will submit the form and find out the box meant no.

## The question lives on `FieldGroup`

A lone checkbox carries its own label and that is enough. A set of them answering one question needs
that question said out loud, and a checkbox does not say it. Wrap them in `FieldGroup`, which
renders a real `<fieldset>` and `<legend>` and announces the question once instead of once per box.
