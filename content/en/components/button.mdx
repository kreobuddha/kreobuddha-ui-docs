## `loading` or `disabled`

Both dim the button and both refuse the click, which is why they are so often used
interchangeably — and why choosing wrong is easy to miss.

A disabled button cannot be focused. For an action that is already under way, that is exactly
wrong: a keyboard user who pressed Enter loses focus mid-action and is left with no idea where they
are. `loading` keeps the button in the tab order, keeps its box the same size so nothing shifts
underneath the pointer, and reports `aria-busy`.

Use `disabled` for an action that cannot be performed, and `loading` for one that is being
performed.

## Keyboard and screen readers

It is a native `<button>`, so activation with Enter and Space, the tab order and the announced role
all come from the platform. `loading` is announced through `aria-busy`; the label does not change,
because a label that changes under a screen reader mid-action reads as a different button.

Both dimmed states keep their contrast: `loading` is dimmed less than `disabled`, so the spinner
stays above the contrast the library asks of a status mark.
