Everything a `<div>` accepts is accepted too, except `children` and `role`.

## Leaving `value` out is a decision

Indeterminate is not a fallback for a number you have not fetched yet. It reports no
`aria-valuenow` at all, which is exactly what stops a screen reader announcing a percentage nobody
measured.

```tsx
<Progress label="Uploading files" value={uploaded} max={total} />

<Progress label="Checking the licence" />
```

The second one says work is under way and admits it cannot say how much is left. That admission is
the feature. A bar that crawls to 90% and waits there has told the reader something false.

## Why this is a `div` and not `<progress>`

The project's usual rule is platform semantics before ARIA, and this is the documented exception.

A native `<progress>` costs three vendor pseudo-element sets — `::-webkit-progress-bar`,
`::-webkit-progress-value`, `::-moz-progress-bar` — to reach the same bar in every engine. A `div`
with `role="progressbar"` costs one styling model. What it buys in CSS it owes in semantics, so
every attribute is asserted in the library's own tests rather than inherited from the element.

## Not a spinner

A spinner says something is happening. This says how much of it has happened, and it is worth its
space only when that number is real. If the number is invented to fill the bar, a spinner is the
honest component.
