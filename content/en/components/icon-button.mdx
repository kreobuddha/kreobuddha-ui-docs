Everything a `<button>` accepts is accepted too, except `children`.

## Why it is not `Button` without a label

The thing that makes an icon button dangerous is exactly the thing a shared component could not
enforce. An icon carries no text, so there is nowhere for an accessible name to come from — and a
`Button` that merely *allows* an empty label will eventually ship with one.

<Callout tone="info" title="label is required and the compiler checks it.">
  It is also used as the hover tooltip, because a symbol without a name is a guess for sighted
  readers too.
</Callout>

## `loading` keeps the size

The spinner replaces the icon inside the same square. Nothing reflows, so a row of controls does not
jump while one of them is working, and a reader's pointer stays over the thing they were aiming at.

The button stays focusable while loading and reports `aria-busy`. Removing it from the tab order
would move focus somewhere else mid-action, which is the one thing a reader cannot undo.

## `disabled` or `loading`

`disabled` for an action that is unavailable. `loading` for one that is already under way. They look
similar and mean opposite things: the first says "not now", the second says "already".

## `xs` is for marks inside another control

24px, small enough to sit inside something else. It is how `Alert` and `Dialog` get their close
buttons, and it is the size to reach for when the button is part of another component rather than a
control in its own right.
