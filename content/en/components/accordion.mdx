## Nothing here manages open state

The platform supplies all of it: the disclosure button, `Enter` and `Space`, the
expanded/collapsed announcement, and — through the shared `name` — the exclusive behaviour.

Where `name` is not implemented, the sections simply open independently. That is a weaker version of
the same component rather than a broken one, which is the test worth applying to any platform
feature before depending on it.

## There is deliberately no height animation

A `<details>` cannot be animated without taking its state back from the browser, and that state is
the whole thing worth having. The trade is stated rather than hidden: sections snap open, and in
exchange the keyboard behaviour, the announcement and the exclusive grouping are the platform's and
cannot drift from it.

## Content stays in the DOM while closed

Which means find-in-page can reach it, and anything expensive inside it is paid for whether the
reader opens the section or not. If the content is expensive, that is an argument for not putting
it here — not for unmounting it, which would take the state back again.
