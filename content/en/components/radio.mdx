One option in a choice where exactly one answer is possible.

**A radio never brings its own question.** It renders no fieldset and no legend, so the playground
below shows what it is always used inside.

Everything an `<input>` accepts is accepted too, except `type` and `size`.

## `name` is required here and optional in HTML

That difference is deliberate. The shared `name` is what makes options one group, and the group is
where all the behaviour worth having lives: arrows move through it and select as they go, `Tab`
treats the whole thing as one stop, and the chosen option is what the form submits.

A radio without a name is not a smaller radio — it is a control that can be turned on and never off
again. The compiler refuses it rather than letting that reach a reader.

## A lone radio is almost always wrong

One option, on its own, that cannot be unset. Interfaces rarely mean that. When one thing is on or
off, the component is `Checkbox`, or `Switch` if it takes effect immediately.

## Where the fault goes

A fault in one option belongs to that option. A fault in the *choice* — "pick one to continue" —
belongs to the group, and putting it on each option means the reader hears it three times for one
mistake.

```tsx
<FieldGroup legend="Deploy target" error="Pick a target before deploying.">
  <Radio name="target" value="production" label="Production" />
  <Radio name="target" value="staging" label="Staging" />
</FieldGroup>
```
