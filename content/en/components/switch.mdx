A setting that takes effect when it is flipped.

That timing is the only thing separating it from a checkbox, and it is a promise to the reader: if
flipping this does nothing until a Save button is pressed, this is the wrong component.

Everything an `<input>` accepts is accepted too, except `type` and `size`.

## `Switch` or `Checkbox`

Not a matter of taste. Ask when the change lands.

Flipping a switch does the thing — the setting is saved, the feature turns on, the request goes
out. Ticking a checkbox records an intention that a submit button will act on later. Readers have
learned both, and a switch that waits for a Save button breaks a promise they did not know they
were relying on.

If the change can fail, the switch has to survive that: it flips back, and something says why.

## Underneath it is a checkbox

An `<input type="checkbox">` carrying `role="switch"`. What is announced becomes "on" and "off"
rather than "checked", while native keyboard handling and form participation stay exactly where
they were.

There is deliberately no `required`. A switch is always in one of its two states, so demanding one
of them is a rule about the value, not about the control — and that rule belongs to the form.
