The question a set of controls answers together, as a real `<fieldset>` and `<legend>`.

Without it, a screen reader reaching a column of checkboxes hears the options and never the
question.

Everything a `<fieldset>` accepts is accepted too, `ref` included.

## The decision it forces

Where a message belongs, and that is the useful part.

Guidance and faults about the **set** go on the group: "choose at least one", "pick a target before
deploying". A fault in one control stays on that control: "this address is not valid".

`aria-describedby` sits on the fieldset rather than on each child, so group guidance is read once
instead of once per option. Put it on every child instead and a reader arrowing through five
options hears the same sentence five times.

## It is what makes `Radio` usable

A radio deliberately renders no question of its own. Every group of radios therefore needs this
component, and that is not an accident of the API — it is the reason both exist in this shape.

Checkboxes are different: one on its own is complete, and it needs a group only when several of
them answer one question together.

## `disabled` is the platform's

A disabled fieldset disables every control inside it. That is native behaviour, inherited rather
than reimplemented per child — which means it also works for controls this library does not own,
including anything an application drops in.
