One panel visible at a time, for views that belong together and are not read together.

There is no native element for this, so the WAI-ARIA tab pattern is implemented in full: the whole
tab list is a single tab stop, arrows move within it, `Home` and `End` jump to the ends, and only
the selected panel is rendered.

## `automatic` or `manual`

This is the decision a consumer has to make, and it is about cost rather than taste.

`automatic` selects as the arrow moves. It is fewer keystrokes and what WAI-ARIA recommends when
panels are cheap. `manual` moves focus only and waits for Enter or Space — for a panel expensive
enough that arrowing past four of them would fire four requests.

## Not for navigation

This switches views. It does not navigate routes: the library owns no routing, and a tab that
changes the URL is a set of links wearing a tab's clothes. Keyboard behaviour, browser history and
the meaning of a middle click all differ, and readers notice.
