## `Alert` or `Toast`

A toast confirms something that already worked and then leaves. An alert is for something the
reader has to read, act on, or come back to.

That splits cleanly on one question: can this be missed? Anything that must not be cannot be
allowed to disappear on a timer, and a reader who looked away for two seconds has missed a toast
with no way to get it back.

## `live` is off on purpose

A banner already on screen when the page loads has nothing to announce. Turning it on anyway means
a screen reader interrupts to read something that was simply there, which is noise the reader did
not ask for.

Turn it on for a message that appears in answer to something the reader did:

```tsx
<Alert tone="danger" live>
  The changes were not saved. The connection dropped part way through.
</Alert>
```

The role follows the tone, and that is deliberate: `danger` interrupts, and the quieter tones wait
for a pause. Interrupting for a success message spends the reader's attention on news that could
have waited.

## The tone is not the message

Each tone supplies a mark as well as a colour, so the meaning survives monochrome and survives a
reader who does not see the hue. Both are backstops. The wording still has to carry it alone: an
alert reading "Deployment" tells no one whether it went well.
